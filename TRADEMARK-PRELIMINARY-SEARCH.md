# Preliminary trademark and name screen — LANGUAGE MINER

Search date: 2026-08-15  
Territory emphasized: United States, with general web/common-law observations

## Result

No decisive clearance conclusion can be made from a preliminary search. The phrase “Language Miner” combines ordinary/descriptive words and sits in a crowded field containing “word miner,” “vocabulary miner,” and “language mining” uses. That may make exclusivity narrower and makes similarity searching especially important.

The source package contains no textual reference to Duolingo and no evidence of a copied Duolingo name or mascot. That does not resolve similarity to every third-party mark, character, interface, lesson, or advertisement.

## Searches performed

- Exact and similar wording on the public web: `"Language Miner"`, `"Language Miner" app`, language-learning “miner” games, “Vocabulary Miner,” and “Japanese Word Miner.”
- Public-source review for use in language-learning software and educational services.
- Source-code text screen for `Duolingo`, its mascot name, and obvious brand references.

Examples of neighboring use found include Vocabulary Miner and Japanese Word Miner. These are not determinations of registration status or legal conflict; they show why a professional similarity search is still needed.

## Required before filing or major paid promotion

1. Have a trademark attorney search exact, phonetic, spelling, translation, design, and commercial-impression variants.
2. Search USPTO federal records, U.S. state records, common-law web use, company names, app stores, domains, and relevant foreign registers.
3. Review at least Nice Classes 9 (downloadable software), 41 (education/entertainment), and 42 (hosted software), plus any merchandise classes actually planned.
4. Compare the full logo and trade dress, not only the words.
5. Preserve dated first-use evidence and do not use the ® symbol unless a registration actually issues. Use of ™ should be discussed with counsel.
